# Google Business Profile — the setup pack

Everything to paste in, in the order to do it. About 90 minutes once, then ten
minutes a week.

When someone in Raipur searches *driver on hire near me*, Google shows a map with
three business pins **above** the website links. Those pins come from a Business
Profile, not from a website — so no amount of work on thedrivebuddy.in can win
that spot. This is the biggest thing left to do for DriveBuddy.

Start at <https://business.google.com/> with the Google account you want to own
this forever (not a staff member's personal account).

---

## Two rules that get profiles suspended

Both are tempting, both are fatal, and a suspended profile is hard to get back.

**1. The name must be exactly `DriveBuddy`.** Not "DriveBuddy Driver on Hire",
not "DriveBuddy Raipur", not "DriveBuddy — Premium Driver Services". Google's
guidelines say the name must be your real-world name with nothing added, and that
putting services or a city in it "could result in the suspension of your Business
Profile." The city goes in the service-area field. The services go in the
services field. They have their own boxes; using them is what makes you rank.

**2. Never invent an address.** You visit the customer, so you are what Google
calls a *service-area business*: you enter your real address for verification,
then **hide it** so it never shows publicly. A rented mailbox, a virtual office,
or a friend's shop address is grounds for removal. A home address is fine and
normal here — hidden, nobody sees it.

## Step 1 — Create it

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| Business name | `DriveBuddy` |
| Primary category | `Chauffeur service` |
| Additional categories | `Transportation service`, `Car service` |
| Do you serve customers at your address? | **No** — you go to them |
| Address | Your real address, then tick **hide my address** |
| Service areas | Raipur, Bhilai, Durg (see the Bilaspur note below) |
| Phone | `+91 91114 73929` |
| Second phone | `+91 98933 02783` |
| Website | `https://thedrivebuddy.in` |
| Hours | Open 24 hours, all seven days |
| Appointment link | `https://thedrivebuddy.in/book/` |

The primary category is the one that decides which searches you appear in, so it
matters more than everything else on this page combined. `Chauffeur service` is
the right one: a driver you hire who drives *your* car. Do **not** pick `Taxi
service` or `Car rental` — you supply no vehicle, and a category that misdescribes
the business is a policy violation as well as the wrong audience.

You may set up to 20 service areas. Add the smaller places you genuinely cover
too — Charoda, Kumhari, Bhilai-3, Patan, Abhanpur, Nava Raipur, Mandir Hasaud.
Each one is a search you can appear in. Only list places you will actually drive
to; padding the list weakens all of them.

### About Bilaspur — read this one

Google's guideline for a service-area business is one profile per **staffed**
location, and a service area that "normally should not cover more than about two
hours' drive." Raipur to Bhilai and Durg is well inside that. Raipur to Bilaspur
is roughly 120 km and two and a half to three hours, so it sits outside it.

There is also a practical reason, separate from the rule: Google ranks map results
mostly by how close the business is to the person searching. A profile based in
Raipur will almost never appear for someone searching in Bilaspur, however many
service areas you list.

So:

- **Now:** one profile, service areas Raipur / Bhilai / Durg and the towns between.
- **When you have someone actually based in Bilaspur:** create a *second* profile
  for that location, with its own hidden address and its own phone if you have
  one, and point its website field at `https://thedrivebuddy.in/cities/bilaspur/`.
  That is legitimate and it is how multi-city service businesses do it.
- **Never:** a second profile at an address where nobody works. That is the exact
  pattern Google's spam team looks for, and it can take down profile one as well.

The `/cities/bilaspur/` page keeps working for ordinary web searches in the
meantime. It is only the map pins you cannot have yet.

## Step 2 — Verification

A service-area business is usually asked for **video verification**: one
continuous recording, no cuts, no pausing, filmed on your phone. Rehearse it once
before you start, because a failed attempt is slow to retry.

Film, in one take:

1. A street sign or recognisable landmark near where you are based, so the
   location is provable.
2. Anything branded — a DriveBuddy visiting card, a driver ID card, a shirt, a
   sticker.
3. The tools of the work: car keys, the driver ID cards, the breathalyser, your
   phone showing the bookings coming in.
4. Yourself, saying your name and that you manage DriveBuddy.

Approval usually takes up to five working days. Until it arrives, nothing you add
is public — keep filling the profile in anyway, it all goes live together.

## Step 5 — Photos

Profiles with real photos get noticeably more calls, and this is where most local
businesses give up. Aim for twelve to start, then two or three a month forever.

Upload in this order:

1. **Logo** — square, at least 720×720.
2. **Cover** — landscape, at least 1080 wide. A driver standing beside a customer's
   car works better than a graphic.
3. **Ten or more real photographs**, ideally: a driver in uniform receiving keys
   from a customer; a driver at the wheel of a customer's car; a night pickup with
   the car lit up; the breathalyser check before a shift; a driver ID card (blur
   the licence number); an airport drop with luggage; a wedding or event duty; an
   elderly passenger being helped into a car; your team together; the phone showing
   a booking coming in.

What matters:

- **Real photographs of your own work.** Stock images of American limousines are
  obvious, and they are the most common reason a profile feels fake.
- **No text plastered across them,** no phone numbers, no borders, no logos in the
  corner. Google demotes photos that look like advertisements.
- **Faces need consent.** Ask the customer before you post their photo. Their car's
  number plate is identifying too — blur it unless it is your own vehicle.
- Geotagging the files does nothing. Google strips that data on upload. Ignore any
  blog that tells you otherwise.

## Step 6 — Reviews

Reviews are the second-strongest thing after the category, and the only part of
this that cannot be done in an afternoon.

**Get your link.** On the profile, **Ask for reviews** gives a short link like
`https://g.page/r/…/review`. Save it. Print it as a QR code on a small card and
give every driver a stack.

**How to ask.** At the drop-off, while the customer is still standing there and
still pleased, the driver hands over the card and says one line: *"If the drive was
good, a review on Google really helps us."* That is it. Asking in person converts
several times better than a message sent later, and asking at the end of the job
beats asking the next day.

**What never to do:**

- Do not offer a discount, cashback or a free hour for a review. Google's review
  markup guidelines were updated on 24 July 2026 to name incentivised reviews
  explicitly, and undisclosed incentives can now draw a manual action.
- Do not write reviews yourself, or have staff and family write them. It is the
  single easiest thing for Google to detect — same device, same network, same day.
- Do not buy them. Thirty reviews in a week on a new profile reads as purchased and
  gets the profile stripped or suspended.

**A realistic target:** ten reviews in the first month, then five to eight a month,
steadily. Steady beats sudden.

**Reply to every single one, within a day.** Replies are public and they are read
by the next customer far more than the review itself.

- Five stars: *"Thank you [name] — glad the drive went smoothly. We'll tell
  [driver] you said so."* Name the driver. It reads as a real business.
- One star: *"I'm sorry, this isn't the standard we hold. Please call me on
  +91 91114 73929 and I'll look into what happened on your booking."* Never argue,
  never blame the customer, never explain at length in public. Take it to a phone
  call.

## Step 9 — The switches everyone forgets

- **Attributes.** Tick "Online appointments" and "On-site services". If it applies,
  "Wheelchair accessible" and "Identifies as woman-owned" are both filters people
  actually search by.
- **Opening date.** Set it to 2024, matching the website.
- **Chat / messaging.** Only turn this on if somebody will reply within 24 hours.
  Google shows a "typically replies in…" time publicly, and a slow one costs you
  more than having no chat at all. If in doubt, leave it off — the call and WhatsApp
  buttons already work.
- **Booking link.** `https://thedrivebuddy.in/book/` under appointment links.
- **Add all four managers.** Profile → Business Profile settings → People and
  access. If one Google account owns this and that account is lost, the profile is
  gone. Add a second owner today.

## What I need to do on the website afterwards

Two things, and both need you:

**1. The profile link.** Once verified, the profile has a share link that looks like
`https://g.page/something`. Send it to me and I will put it in `lib/site.ts`, where
it flows into the structured data on every page as an official profile of the same
business. That is how you get Google to treat the website and the map listing as
one company rather than two — it is the strongest single signal for making a
"DriveBuddy" panel appear on the right-hand side of search results.

**2. The 4.9 from 187 reviews — this needs deciding.** `lib/site.ts` currently
declares a 4.9 average from 187 reviews, and the website publishes that to Google
as machine-readable review data. It was carried over from the old site. Two
problems:

- It earns nothing. Google's own documentation says a business publishing ratings
  about itself makes those pages "ineligible for star review feature", and that
  "ratings must be sourced directly from users". Stars will never show from it.
- Since 24 July 2026 the same guidelines say "don't include fake or undisclosed
  incentivized reviews on your page or in your structured data markup", and a
  violation can bring a manual action — after which Google ignores *all* the
  structured data on the page, including the parts doing real work for you.

You have no Google reviews yet, so 187 cannot be verified by anyone. My
recommendation is to delete the number — it is two lines, it changes nothing
visible on the page, and it removes the risk entirely. Real stars will come from
real Google reviews, on the profile, where customers can see them. Say the word
and I will remove it.

The visible "4.9★" on the homepage is a separate thing — ordinary marketing copy,
not review data Google polices. Your call whether to keep it, but I would change it
once you have genuine reviews to point at.
